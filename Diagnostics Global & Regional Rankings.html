01Key findings

Executive summary designed for fast reading before entering detailed rankings.

01

Omnicom Media becomes the new benchmark

Following the integration of OMG and IPG Mediabrands, Omnicom Media leads globally with 34.5% quali-share and ranks first in each region.

02

OMD keeps the network crown

OMD remains #1 for the 11th consecutive edition with 10.5% global quali-share, while Starcom and Zenith are rising behind it.

03

Publicis Media posts the strongest three-year growth

Publicis Media Groupe gained +6.2pts in quali-share over three years, driven by Starcom, Zenith and Spark Foundry momentum.

04

WPP Media continues to decline

WPP Media lost -4.5pts over three years, with EssenceMediacom and Wavemaker particularly affected by major global departures.

05

Starcom leads Vitality while OMD leads Structure

Starcom ranks first on Vitality criteria, helped by new business momentum. OMD leads Structure, reflecting depth of capabilities and client portfolio balance.

06

Regional dynamics vary strongly

North America is led by Spark Foundry at agency level, LATAM by Havas Media, while OMD leads both EMEA and APAC.

The DIAGNOSTICS report
develops a thorough, relevant portrait
of the competition thanks to 19 criteria going well beyond the basic
measure of business volume
This holistic evaluation works as a unique, relevant marketing index that enables
effective comparison of agency performance across multiple identical criteria in
45 countries. The 19 criteria combine both one-year and three-year KPIs -
ensuring the assessment goes beyond short-term analysis - to evaluate not only
each agency’s momentum (Pitches won, New Business Balance, Awards) but also
their structural strength (Specialized Staff and Capabilities, Client Portfolio
Structure).
The goal is not to identify which agency won the most clients lately (that’s the
New Business Balance), nor which is the biggest in size (that’s the Activity
Volume), nor even which is the most competitive in pitches (that’s Compitches).
The objective is to determine which agency performs best overall, demonstrating
both excellence and consistency across all criteria.
The multi-criteria analysis also allows for comparisons between agencies of
similar size, helping to identify differences in their profiles in terms of types of
wins (Global vs. Local), client portfolios (Large vs. Mid-sized, Local vs.
International Clients), and level of diversification (Traditional Media Share vs.
Non-traditional Media). This provides media professionals with a deeper
understanding of the competitive landscape and supports more informed
decision-making.
Thanks to this unique methodology, it’s often clear that the largest agencies in
volume are not necessarily the highest-performing ones at a given time.
The Diagnostics report enables you to assess agency performance according to
this exclusive index, through a wide range of key findings, tables, rankings, trend-
graphs, dashboards and analysis available at global, regional, and local levels.

1. Quali-points
Quali-points are based on the total number of points distributed
to each agency, according to the 18 to 19 KPIs/ criteria in the
Qualitative Evaluation applied in 45 countries.
See definition of each criteria in part 4 Methodology.
Each agency in every coutry is assessed versus its competitors
through each criteria according to a 4-level Strength scale which
provides:
Two points for a strong strength
One point for a light strength
No point for an average score
Minus one point for a weakness
2. Competition Index
This metric provides the distance to the leader.
Network n°1 = index 100; Network n°2= ratio between this n°2
network and the leading network in Quali-Points.
The Competition index enables to easily visualize the hierarchy
between the agencies.
3. Quali-share
The metric is a ratio between an agency’s Quali-points and the
total scores of the 16 networks, by country, region or zone.
A growth in quali-points can be combined with a decrease in
Quali-share if the total of the industry increased more than the
network.
In part one of the report, trend-graphs are in Quali-shares. In part
three, Management Dashboards, in quali-points, provide insights
to each agency trend.

Omnicom Media
Officially created at the end of November 2025 by the integration of Omnicom Media
Group and IPG Mediabrands, Omnicom Media is emerging as the new industry
powerhouse. Leader in Activity Volume/Billings with around 30% industry share,
Omnicom Media also leads this Diagnostic Report globally with just under 35% of quali-
share and, importantly, ranks First in each region.
Omnicom Media is led by Florian Adamski, previously Global CEO of OMG, with several
former IPG Mediabrands executives joining the leadership team, including Justin Wroe,
Global Chief Commercial Officer (ex-CEO of IPG Mediabrands US and ex-Global Chief
Transformation Officer) and Jarrod Martin, Global Chief Transformation Officer (ex-
Global CEO of Kinesso). At regional level, this new group is managed by former OMG top
executives: Ralph Pardo in North America, Dan Clays in EMEA, Tony Harradine in APAC
and Julian Porras in Latam.
Today, Omnicom Media has retained all six media agencies from the two groups: OMD,
PHD and Hearts & Science (ex-OMG), and UM, Initiative and Mediahub (ex-IPG
Mediabrands). This broad offering helps minimize the risk of client conflicts as much as
possible.
In addition to an increased buying power, Omnicom’s acquisition of IPG significantly
enhances Omni, the marketing intelligence platform developed by Omnicom. One of the
major updates announced in January 2026 involves the addition of Acxiom’s data (ex-IPG)
and integration with Flywheel to develop a leading commerce offering. This was a
missing link until now, whereas Publicis, for example, could rely on Epsilon.

RECMA evaluates the client porfolio of each agency according to four criteria:
1.
Big advertisers reveals if the agency is servicing one or several big clients (thresholds
to define Big clients vary from one market to another).
2.
Exposure to top 3 clients reveals how vulnerable the agency may be to its Top 3 clients,
the less dependency they manage to get, the more secure they are.
3.
Local advertisers reveals if an agency relies not only on big advertisers - mostly global
and US - but also on local advertisers measured to a specific threshold by country.
4.
Relationship stability reveals if an agency has stable clients.
Ranking by region for each of these criteria are available in the next pages.
According to these criteria, the biggest agencies on volume and staff are high in the
ranking. Havas Media enters the top agencies as it benefits from its excellent results and
its volume in the Top 5 Europe and Latam. To the exception of Exposure, OMD ranks first
across all client profile criteria at the global level. WPP Media agencies also occupy the
top part of the ranking, except in Big Advertisers, where Mindshare and Wavemaker
have respectively fallen to 7th and 8th places, after being 3rd and 4th three years ago.
Compared to its size, Havas is evaluated at a high level (6th globally) thanks to a low level
of Exposure towards Top 3 clients (1st) and a client list composed of numerous mid-sized
or small local clients (4th)
As these criteria are evolving less quickly than the Vitality criteria (New Business Balance,
or Compitches for example), the hierarchy between the agencies is more established
from one edition to another.

number of top accounts in recent years (Kenvue, J&J USA, Spotify), UM appears to have very limited
exposure to its top clients, while OMD and Initiative sit in the middle of the global ranking. By contrast,
